Privacy Policy

Effective October 19, 2026

Orvix Aero is a pilot logbook app that fills itself in. This policy explains what information the Orvix Aero app and orvixaero.com collect, why, and the choices you have. In short: your logbook belongs to you, we only use your information to run Orvix Aero, and we never sell it.

What we collect

  • Account details. Your email address and a password, or the name and email address that Apple or Google share with us when you choose “Continue with Apple” or “Continue with Google.” If you use Apple’s “Hide My Email,” we only receive Apple’s private relay address.
  • Your pilot profile. Certificates and ratings you add, starting totals from before Orvix Aero, your medical class and dates, and endorsements, including your instructor’s name, certificate number and signature as they entered it.
  • Your logbook. Flights you enter, import or confirm: dates, airports, times, aircraft and tail numbers, landings, remarks and similar logbook entries, plus settings such as your home airport.
  • Location, only if you turn on flight detection. The app uses your phone’s location, including in the background if you allow it, to notice takeoffs and landings and suggest a logbook entry. We keep the route of each detected flight so you can see it on your map. You can turn this off at any time in your phone’s settings.
  • Your schedule, if you add it. Trips and months you upload, paste or enter by hand: dates, flight numbers, airports, scheduled times, block times, trip numbers and duty times. If you upload a screenshot, we send it to be read and don’t keep the image. We never read or store pay, credit, sick time or names from your schedule.
  • Paper logbook scans, if you use them. Photos of logbook pages you take or pick are sent to be read and aren’t kept. The flights you check and save become part of your logbook. Camera and photo access are used only for this.
  • Saved flights and alerts. Flights you save for alerts, and your device’s push token so we can send them.
  • Pay rates stay on your phone. The hourly rate, per diem and guarantee you enter for the pay estimate are stored only on your device and never sent to us.
  • Weather and aircraft lookups. When you look up weather we receive the airport code you searched. When you add an aircraft we look up its public FAA registration record.
  • Waitlist. If you join the waitlist on orvixaero.com, we keep your email address so we can tell you when we launch.

How we use it

  • To sign you in and keep your account secure.
  • To build and show your logbook, totals and flight map.
  • To detect flights and suggest entries for you to confirm. Nothing is added to your logbook until you confirm it.
  • To show weather for the airports you choose.
  • To lay out your schedule, check it against FAR 117, keep your trips up to date with live flight status, and estimate your pay on your phone.
  • To send the flight alerts you turn on.
  • To answer you when you contact us, and to send important account messages such as email confirmations and password resets.

We do not sell your information, use it for advertising, or share your flights with other pilots. We do not show aircraft owner details.

Who helps us run Orvix Aero

We use a small number of service providers who process information only to provide their service to us:

  • Supabase stores your account and logbook data.
  • Apple and Google handle sign-in if you choose them.
  • aviationweather.gov (U.S. National Weather Service) provides weather reports; it receives airport codes, not your identity.
  • Expo delivers app updates and, with Apple, push notifications for flight alerts.
  • Anthropic reads the schedule screenshots and paper logbook photos you upload. Our server sends the image and gets back the trips or flight rows; your name and email aren’t sent with it.
  • AeroDataBox and Aviationstack provide flight status and gates. Our server asks about a flight number, date and airports; your identity isn’t sent.
  • Known Crewmember checkpoint information comes from the public knowncrewmember.org site; only airport codes are looked up.
  • NOAA / National Weather Service provides the radar layer on the map; your phone loads radar images for the area you’re viewing.
  • OpenStreetMap data, served by Geoapify (and Geofabrik's Postpass for gate numbers), provides places around airports. Our server asks for places near an airport; your identity and location are not sent.
  • Near me (optional): when you ask for places around you, the app sends only a rounded location, accurate to about 1 km, to our server, which looks up places around that spot and keeps the results for a week so others nearby can reuse them. Your exact position stays on your phone and is used only to sort the list by distance.

We may disclose information if the law requires it.

Information from Google sign-in

If you sign in with Google, we receive your name, email address and Google account ID, and use them only to create and sign in to your Orvix Aero account. Orvix Aero’s use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Keeping it safe

Data is encrypted in transit, and each pilot can only access their own records. No system is perfectly secure, but we work to protect your information and limit who can access it.

Your choices

  • Export. You can export your whole logbook as a CSV file at any time, for free, from Settings → Your data.
  • Delete. You can delete your account in the app. This permanently deletes your account, logbook, flight routes, schedule, saved flights and settings.
  • Location. You can turn location access off in your phone’s settings; you can still log flights by hand.
  • Questions or requests. Email info@orvixaero.com to ask about, correct or delete your information, or to leave the waitlist.

Children

Orvix Aero is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them.

Changes

If we change this policy we will update the date at the top, and tell you in the app or by email if the change is significant.
